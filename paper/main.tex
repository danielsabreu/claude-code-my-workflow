\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This paper proposes a novel approach to measuring the financial cycle using a dynamic factor model (DFM) that synthesizes information from key financial indicators. We construct country-specific financial cycle indicators for a sample of European economies and show that they provide a coherent measure of cyclical systemic risk. A central contribution is a statistically grounded framework for identifying neutral cyclical risk conditions: drawing on the asymptotic distribution of the principal components estimator, we derive time-varying confidence bands that distinguish periods of elevated, neutral, and subdued financial cycle activity without relying on ad hoc thresholds. This directly supports the operationalisation of the positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer (CCyB), providing a principled, data-driven criterion for when cyclical systemic risk is neither building up nor materialising. We also evaluate the indicator in a pseudo real-time, out-of-sample setting and find that it delivers economically meaningful early-warning signals of financial crises, with the estimated one-year-ahead probability of a crisis approximately doubling---from approximately 10\% in early 2006 to around 20\% by 2007---in the run-up to the Global Financial Crisis. These features, together with a decomposition of the indicator into variable contributions, make the approach a transparent and operational tool for monitoring cyclical systemic risk and informing macroprudential policy.

\end{abstract}
